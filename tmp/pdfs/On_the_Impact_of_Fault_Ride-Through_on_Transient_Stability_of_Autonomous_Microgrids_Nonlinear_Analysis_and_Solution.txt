On the Impact of Fault Ride-Through on Transient
Stability of Autonomous Microgrids: Nonlinear
Analysis and Solution
Mohsen Eskandari and Andrey V . Savkin , Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—Fault ride-through (FRT) is essential for inverter-
interfaced distributed generation (IIDG) units to protect semi-
conductor switches from being imposed to overcurrent conditions
while the transients are securely passed. To this end, a current
limiting strategy is adopted for IIDG units, mostly embedded in
control loops, to limit the current within the withstand-able band
and to make the IIDG units stay connected to the (micro) grid
during the transient. However, the FRT/current limiting of grid-
forming inverters affects the transient stability of the autonomous
droop-based microgrids and may make them unstable, which
yet has not been well-explored in the literature. This issue is
considered in this work through a scrupulous observation of
the second-order nonlinear differential equation describing the
frequency-phase angle dynamics and investigating the problem
through the Lyapunov theory. The Chetaev’s instability theo-
rem, which is developed based on the Lyapunov direct method,
is used to explore the instability conditions due to the FRT. It is
revealed that the phase angle variation, as a consequence of the
current limiting, and the arbitrary/resistive transient impedance
of grid-forming inverters make the system unstable. Numerical
and time-domain results through MATLAB/Simulink platform
prove the validity of the models.
Index Terms —Current limiting, droop control, fault ride-
through (FRT), inverter-interfaced distributed generation (DG)
units, microgrid, transient stability.

A. Control System of Grid-Forming VSIs
A hierarchical control structure is used for autonomous
MGs and the lowest (primary) control level, including the
power (droop) control loop and inner controllers of the
IIDG units, is in charge of securing transient/dynamic sta-
bility of the whole system [36]–[37] while implementing
power-sharing [10] and voltage regulation [11]. The FRT
is accomplished by embedding current limiters in the inner
control loops of IIDG units, as shown in Fig. 1. Normally,
anti-windup PI controllers are adopted to avoid the overvoltage
problem caused by the saturation issue of the current limiter.
The conventional f − P and V − Q droop rules are given as

Concluded from the Lyapunov direct method (Chetaev’s the-
orem) and the developed transient impedance of a grid-forming
VSI, the following statement is released: the arbitrary/resistive
transient impedance of the VSIs combined with the sudden
phase angle variation at the VSI’s output voltage are comple-
mentary parts to realize the Chetaev’s instability condition .
Numerical Example: an autonomous MG, including three
generation buses (VSIs) and one load bus connected through
the power network with a meshed topology, is simulated to
investigate the instability issue. The simulated model and
detailed information are presented in Section IV . A three-phase
to ground fault is simulated in the power network and IIDG
units switch to current limiting mode to preserve the FRT
scheme. Fig. 6(b) shows a zoom-on plot of the frequency and
phase angle variations in the current limiting period, which

theorem. Fig. 6(c) demonstrates the sub-transient and tran-

Since the system response to transients is unpredictable due
to the arbitrary transient impedance of the VSIs, variable struc-

the whole system. To this end, and to stabilize the autonomous
MG in the FRT, the motion of the system might be controlled
along with the sable boundaries. In this regard, sliding mode
control could be effective which has been successfully tested
in the multi-agent (micro) power systems [42]. On the other
hand, power-sharing and synchronism of the droop controllers
must be guaranteed in designing the sliding mode control.

V. C
ONCLUSION
The impact of current limiting, which is executed to realize
the fault ride-through of IIDG units, on the transient stability
of autonomous MGs was investigated in this work. To this
end, the nonlinear second-order differential equation describ-
ing the dynamics of the f − P droop loop was developed and
stability analysis has been conducted through the Lyapunov
theory. It was demonstrated that the Lyapunov function, in
its general sense in the power systems, cannot provide a suf-
ﬁcient stability condition for inverter-based MGs. Using the
Chetaev’s instability theorem the condition for instability of
the system was explored and the transient impedance of the
VSI was developed to investigate if the FRT of the grid-
forming VSIs satisﬁes the instability condition. It has been
proved that FRT makes the autonomous MGs unstable due to
arbitrary/resistive output impedance of VSIs in current lim-
iting in completion with the sudden phase angle variation at
the VSI’s output voltage which is against the performance of
the f − P droop controllers and satisﬁes Chetaev’s instability
condition.
Finally, an appropriate solution to the transient stability
problem of the autonomous MGs was proposed employing
sliding mode control and validated through simulation results.
The robustness and effectiveness of the proposed method was
compared with the existing technique in [31] which proved
the superiority of the proposed method in this article.
